## 1. The 2027 call: facts and quotes

### 1.1 What was checked
- The news item "Showcase your research at Science is Wonderful! 2027" was published on 15 Sep 2026 [S1]. It links to only three relevant pages: the EUSurvey **application form** [S2], the "Learn more about Science is Wonderful!" page [S4], and a 2025 Horizon Magazine article [S11].
- **There is no separate call text, guidelines PDF, FAQ or info session** linked from the 2027 call (checked 2026-10-01). All detailed rules are inside the EUSurvey form [S2]. A 2027 privacy statement is attached to the form [S3].
- The form's question texts load through JavaScript. I retrieved them from the form's own element endpoint (`/eusurvey/runner/elements/1137942`) [S2], so the field list below is complete.
- The ERC runs a separate call for ERC grantees for the same 2027 fair [S21]. It is not relevant to the DCs.

### 1.7 Equipment, safety, children, photography and data: what the official texts do and do not say
- **Equipment and safety:** The only provisions are the special-requirements list (including "Dangerous substances") and the organisers' right to check feasibility and suggest adjustments [S2]. There are **no published rules on robots, moving machines, electrical safety, lasers or batteries**. **UNVERIFIED**: ask the organisers after selection.
- **Children:** The texts give no safeguarding or child-protection rules for exhibitors. Visitors are school groups with teachers. In 2026, schools and groups of 10 or more had to pre-register, and families could attend without registering [S5].
- **Photography and recording by the EC:** The 2027 privacy statement (record DPR-EC-1063.1) covers live web-streaming, audio-visual recording, photos of speakers and panoramic photos of participants. *"Explicit consent from participants will be sought and treated through a specific separate consent form whenever they appear on publicly published pictures and audiovisual recordings."* There is also an EC photo booth (images kept 7 days) [S3].
- **The lead researcher's video** *"will be used purely for the selection procedure and will not be shared outside the European Commission's staff"* [S3].
- **Recording or processing visitors' data at our booth is not covered by any call text.** The EC privacy statement covers only the EC's own processing [S3]. Our institution (SUT) would be responsible for any camera or microphone processing at the booth. This is an inference from [S3]. Legal analysis belongs to the GDPR track.

### 1.8 Support and budget
- Travel, accommodation and meals for up to 3 people and up to 4 nights. More people only with justification [S1][S2].
- *"The Commission can also help you purchase or ship any approved material you need to present your work… we cannot pay for 'goodies' or giveaways. That being said, teams are welcome to bring their own materials."* [S2] There is no published cap or process. **UNVERIFIED** whether a robot dog or laptop would count as "approved material". Do not plan the hardware budget around it.

**AI and robotics examples:**
- 2026, ERC booth 17, "Heart to heart: how good is AI?" (Univ. Bordeaux): a "BEAT-AI game" where visitors compete against AI at "spotting the heart's chambers", plus VR heart exploration [S10]. This is the closest precedent to ours: human versus AI, with the AI's performance made visible.

**What worked** (organisers and researchers):
- Modular activities [S2].
- Hands-on work, manual activity and all the senses [S2].
- Physical challenges with queues: the drone [S11].
- A familiar opening that leads to the unknown: "I start by asking what sounds animals make… Then I ask them what sound bacteria make" [S7].
- Explanation through interpreters is hard: "Sometimes we are working with two interpreters at the same time" [S7].

**Interaction duration:** The official figure is **about 15 minutes per group** [S1][S2]. I found no per-child figure.

- About 4,000–4,500 pupils, about 40 booths, 16 public hours [S2][S8].

## 4. Conclusions for the proposal

### 4.1 What to emphasise, mapped to the criteria
1. **Feasibility** (our top priority, and the first criterion) [S1]:
   - Show a concrete, low-risk set-up: off-the-shelf robot, one laptop, and the TV screen the fair provides [S2].
   - Everything runs offline.
   - A static or slow-moving robot inside a marked zone.
   - A fallback mode: the robot is off and the webcam-only demo still teaches the same lesson.
   - The jury flags "a big gap between the proposal and its delivery" [S1]. Only promise what you will build.
2. **Suitability** [S1][S2]:
   - Interactive and modular, for groups of 7–18-year-olds and whole classes, within 15 minutes.
   - Manual and multi-sense: physical sensor switches, "blindfold the robot", "earmuffs for the robot".
   - Language-light, with pictograms and an EN/FR/NL interface.
3. **Impact** [S1]:
   - Relevant to children's lives: phones, cameras, smart speakers, "what can a machine know about me?".

3. **Redesign for groups, within 15 minutes** [S2]:
   - **0–2 min, hook:** "Which senses do you use to recognise a friend?" Then reveal the robot's "senses": camera, microphone, plus others if available.
   - **2–11 min, three volunteer rounds of about 3 minutes each.** In each round one child interacts with the robot. The rest of the group:
     - (a) operates big **physical sensor switches** (camera, mic, other) and a "robot blindfold" or "earmuffs";
     - (b) predicts on a vote card what the robot will conclude and how sure it will be.
     - The screen shows the robot's evolving "picture of you" with confidence bars and "because…" evidence icons per sensor.

## 6. Pitfalls and what not to do

- **Do not miss the deadline: 6 Oct 2026, 12:00 CEST** [S1]. EUSurvey uploads have a 1 MB limit [S2]. Do not try to upload the video. Use a link.
- **Do not use the TUAI acronym or jargon in the title** ("Resist that temptation, you loveable nerd") [S2].
- **Do not design for one child at a time** when groups of up to a whole class come for about 15 minutes [S2].
- **Do not rely on venue Wi-Fi or cloud APIs.** Run offline, for robustness and for privacy.
- **Do not rely on speech recognition.** A hall with thousands of pupils is loud, and visitors speak mostly FR or NL [S1]. Use loudness, claps or rhythm, or a close microphone. Treat noise as a teaching moment about uncertainty.
- **Do not let the robot walk freely among children** in a 4×2 m booth. Only 3 bigger booths exist [S2].
- **Do not infer emotions, age, gender, ethnicity or identity.**
  - The AI Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689) prohibits AI that infers emotions "in the areas of workplace and education institutions" (Art. 5(1)(f)) [L1].
  - It also prohibits biometric categorisation that deduces "race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, sex life or sexual orientation" (Art. 5(1)(g)) [L1].
  - Both have applied since 2 Feb 2025 [L2].
  - [corrected by verifier: the consolidated text on [L1] now carries amendments. Points (f) and (g) keep their letters, but new points 5(1)(ba) (non-consensual intimate imagery) and 5(1)(bb) (CSAM generation) apply from 2 Dec 2026. Art. 113 now also staggers the high-risk dates (Annex III from 2 Dec 2027, Annex I from 2 Aug 2028). Art. 50 transparency obligations have applied since 2 Aug 2026: Art. 50(1) requires providers to make sure people know they are interacting with an AI system, and Art. 50(3) requires deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems to inform exposed persons. A visible "this robot uses AI" notice at the booth is therefore advisable. Cite the EUR-Lex consolidated version in the legal track.]
  - Whether a school-group visit to a fair counts as "education institutions" is unclear, so avoid the whole category. Details belong to the legal track.
- **Do not record, store or photograph visitors** with the demo. The EC's consent forms cover only the EC's own photos and recordings [S3].
- **Do not promise more than you can build.** The jury compares the proposal with the video for exactly this gap [S1].
- **Do not assume the EC buys the robot.** "Approved material" support is discretionary [S2].
- **Batteries:** Under IATA 2026 passenger guidance, lithium-ion batteries over 160 Wh must be shipped as dangerous-goods cargo, and 100–160 Wh spares need operator approval [L3]. [corrected by verifier: in IATA Table 1, batteries of >100 to ≤160 Wh need operator approval **both when installed in a device and as spares**. Spares are carry-on only, never in checked baggage. Power banks in that range are "Forbidden". Spares of ≤100 Wh are also carry-on only.] Robot-dog packs may exceed 160 Wh (**UNVERIFIED** per model; hardware track). Plan road or rail transport Gliwice–Brussels, or EC-arranged shipping.
- **Do not forget an unpowered fallback:** printed "sensor cards" and a game of combining evidence by hand, so the booth works even if the robot fails.
