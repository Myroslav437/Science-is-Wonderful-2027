## 5. B2 How do you plan on presenting your research? (max 3,000 characters)

<!-- id:B2 -->
```text
Our booth is a 15-minute group game where pupils become the scientists. A small robot dog sits still in a pen on the table, at children's eye height. Its camera is its "eyes" and a hand-held "dog-bone" microphone is its "ears". The TV shows what the robot believes about one volunteer: a cartoon in which every part (favourite animal and colour, clothes colour, glasses, how much is happening right now, how loud the voice is right now) stays faded with a "?" until the robot has evidence, then gets a badge for the sense that found it.

Start (2 min). Which senses do you use to recognise a friend? The robot says it is a computer program, not a pet. Our promise: its senses stay off until you switch them on, and it forgets you when you leave.

Volunteer rounds (2-3 x 3 min). One pupil stands on the mat. The group predicts with vote cards what the robot will get right. The volunteer chooses to switch on EYES, then EARS. The robot asks two questions: favourite animal (say it or show a picture card) and favourite colour. "Why" cards appear: "I heard 'chien'", "I saw blue on your top". Then the group flips big switches: eyes off, ears off, foggy lens, noisy room. They watch the robot go from "I'm sure" to "I don't know" and check their predictions. Finally: fool the robot! Show a cat card but say "dog". The senses disagree, and the robot has to say so.

Reveal (2 min). Which sense helped most? When was the robot sure but wrong? We open "Things I will never guess, and why": who you are, your feelings, age, gender, where you come from, your health.

Forget me (1 min). A pupil presses the button and the robot visibly wipes its memory.

Our research is the core. Pi-Wei's PhD is sensor fusion: combining cameras and other sensors so robots, like self-driving factory vehicles, keep working when one sensor fails and know when they are unsure. Myroslav's PhD is explainable AI: making an AI show why it decided something. The switches create sensor failures, the certainty words show uncertainty, the "why" cards are explanations, not invented afterwards: each card shows the real numbers the robot used.

For every age. 7-10: icons, the cartoon, eyes and ears. 11-14: the clue balance (how much each sense pushed the answer) and certainty words. 15-18: the maths (adding up clues), bias, and why European law forbids some AI guesses.

Languages. Pupils pick French, Dutch or English with a flag button; the robot listens for answers in all three. Signs and screen texts are trilingual, and the fair's language assistants help us.

Privacy. Everything runs offline on our own laptop. Nothing is recorded or saved, the camera only looks at the volunteer mat, and the screen shows a cartoon, never a clear image of your face. Anyone can play without the camera.

Our prototype already builds this picture, with certainty words and "why" cards, from recordings of real people. By March we connect it to the robot and rehearse the full activity.
```

### 9.2 Filming tips

- Phone in landscape, at eye level (books or a tripod), about 1-1.5 m away. Face the window so daylight is on your face, not behind you.
- Quiet room, door closed, phone close to you. One clean take is better than an edited one; 2-3 attempts are normal.
- Props: a toy dog, or the PiDog if it is already assembled (the Pi 5 4 GB is only due ~13 Nov 2026, brief §3), and two A4 cards with large icons and words **"EYES"** and **"EARS"**.
- Speak slowly and smile. Short sentences. You may read from a sheet behind the phone, but look at the lens.
- Do not film children or other people. No music needed.
- Trim to 120 s or less. Check the sound on headphones.

## 12. Ethics statement (no form field; keep ready)

Brief §7 asks for an ethics statement based on research/09 §13, and the research/09 §12 timeline says "6 Oct 2026: submit the proposal with the ethics statement". The form has no field for it: it does not ask for a data-protection plan, and the organisers ask for the exact requirements only after selection (research/01 §1.6) [S2]. So:

- **In the form:** B2 "Start" (senses off until switched on, the robot forgets you), "Reveal" (things it will never guess) and "Privacy" (offline, nothing saved, camera on the mat only, cartoon display, play without the camera), plus one B9 sentence on the DPIA with the SUT DPO.
- **Ready to send:** the paragraph below, for the post-selection requirements, the organisers' school information and the DPIA file.

Adapted from research/09 §13. Changes: future tense for the DPIA and staff vetting (neither exists on 2 Oct 2026, per the research/09 wording note), the signals listed are our 6 slots (brief §1), the adult-present rule (research/09 §10) is added, and the removed hat detector is named (brief §1).

> "How does a robot see you?" is designed to be privacy-protective and child-safe from the start. The robot's camera and microphone stay off until the volunteer chooses to switch them on, and they can be switched off at any time; pupils can also play without camera or microphone. Camera and microphone are used only while the group's teacher or another accompanying adult is present. All processing happens live on a local, offline computer: no images, sound or transcripts are recorded, nothing leaves the booth, and the robot's memory is wiped when the volunteer leaves. The robot uses only simple, non-sensitive signals: the colour of clothes, whether someone wears glasses, how much someone moves and how loud they speak right now, and the answers a pupil chooses to say or show (a favourite animal and colour). It never tries to identify anyone and never guesses emotions, age, gender, ethnicity, health or personality. We built a hat detector during development and removed it after our ethics review, because headwear can reveal religion. Explaining why AI should not make such guesses is part of what visitors learn. The robot tells visitors in child-friendly French, Dutch and English that it is a computer program without feelings. We do not photograph pupils. All booth staff will be trained in, and bound by, our university's child-protection standards, and will be vetted under Polish child-protection law before the fair. The exhibit is designed to comply with the GDPR, the EU AI Act (including the Article 5 prohibitions and the Article 50 transparency duties), UNICEF's Guidance on AI and Children and our university's data-protection procedures. Before the fair it will undergo a data protection impact assessment reviewed by the Data Protection Officer of the Silesian University of Technology. No research data is collected at the fair.

Basis: research/09 §10 (opt-in, no-camera mode, adult present), §12 (DPIA with the DPO, register and criminal-record checks before the fair, no research data at SiW), §13 (base text), §15 ("say 'designed to comply with'", no photos of pupils); brief §1 and §7.
