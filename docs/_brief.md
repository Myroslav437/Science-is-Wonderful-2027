## 1. Concept (final)
- Title: "How does a robot see you?"
- A small robot dog senses a volunteer with its camera ("eyes") and microphone ("ears"). Its AI builds an internal **representation**: a belief (probability distribution) over **6 child-safe "slots"**, each with evidence and uncertainty. Visitors flip big physical switches (EYES / EARS, plus "foggy lens" and "noisy room" trouble switches) and see the representation and its explanation change in real time.
- Educational message: "The robot does not know you. It builds a picture from incomplete sensor evidence. Different senses give different evidence; combining them helps; noise and missing senses make it unsure; it can be confidently wrong; an explanation shows which evidence drove each conclusion; and some things it must never guess."
- The 6 slots (closed vocabulary; register in prototype/robotsees/config.py):
  1. favourite animal (12 values) - EARS: word said (strong); EYES: an animal picture card/toy shown (moderate).
  2. favourite colour (11 basic colours) - EARS: colour word said (strong); EYES: clothes colour as a weak hint (relevance 0.35).
  3. clothes colour - EYES only (k-means in CIELAB on the upper chest, 11 colour names).
  4. glasses yes/no - EYES only (CLIP contrast prompts, calibrated).
  5. "how much is happening right now" (still / moving / very active) - EYES: body-keypoint speed; EARS: sound level. A MOMENTARY READING, never a trait.
  6. "voice right now" (quiet / normal / loud) - EARS: loudness vs booth calibration. Momentary reading.
- Never inferred (shown on screen as "Things I will never guess, and why"): identity/face or voice recognition, emotion, age, gender, ethnicity/skin tone, hair colour, height/body shape, health/disability, accent/native language, headwear (can reveal religion), personality/interaction style, attention/interest. Hat detection was built and then REMOVED after the ethics review (research/09-ethics-law.md, attribute register). Speaking speed is not labelled (unfair to stutterers/second-language speakers).
- What changed vs the original idea (must be stated explicitly in the proposal):
  1. 3-5 min single visitor -> 15-min group module with 2-3 volunteer rounds of ~3 min while the group operates switches and predicts ("modular", as the call asks).
  2. "AI-generated portrait of the visitor" -> the representation is an interpretable belief state drawn as a procedural cartoon avatar (every element = one slot, faded/dashed/"?" when unsure, badge = which sense found it). The generative image is optional "robot's imagination": LCM-Dreamshaper v7 (OpenVINO int8, MIT licence) restyles the robot's OWN avatar drawing (img2img, strength 0.7, 4 steps) into a cartoon ROBOT mascot, with a prompt made only of closed-vocabulary words the robot believes (guess level or higher) - it never sees the camera image and never draws a human likeness; labelled on screen + machine-readable AI marking (IPTC trainedAlgorithmicMedia + EXIF; AI Act Art. 50(2)). Measured 2.6 s per image on the dev laptop's integrated GPU. An earlier variant (SD-Turbo text-to-image on CPU, 22-35 s) was replaced because it ignored the avatar and drew weak companions.
  3. Inferring "adult/child", age, gender, emotion -> replaced by observable, non-sensitive, momentary signals + volunteered answers ("data you give" vs "data it infers").
  4. Free conversation / LLM dialogue -> scripted robot questions + closed-vocabulary keyword spotting in EN/FR/NL/DE/PL; template explanations (no LLM writes text at runtime).
  5. Robot walking among children -> robot sits on the table in a pen at child eye height, gestures only.
  6. QR code/email delivery -> nothing by default + "forget me" moment; optional offline-printed card with no photo and no name.
  7. Microphone on a moving robot in an 80 dB hall -> hand-held close-talk "dog-bone" microphone (still the robot's "ears"), robot still while listening.

## 2. Group activity (15 min) and volunteer round (~3 min)
- 0-2 min: hook ("How do you recognise a friend? Which senses?"), robot introduces itself as a computer program; privacy promise.
- 2-12 min: 2-3 volunteer rounds of ~3 min: volunteer stands on the mat; group predicts (vote cards); EYES on -> EARS on -> robot asks 2 questions (favourite animal - say it or show a picture card; favourite colour) -> screen shows the avatar + "why" cards -> group flips switches (eyes off / ears off / foggy lens / noisy room) and compares with predictions -> "fool the robot" (show a cat card but say "dog") -> conflict and uncertainty shown.
- 12-14 min: reveal & discuss: what each sense contributed (clue balance), when the robot was wrong, things it will never guess.
- 14-15 min: "forget me" button: the robot visibly wipes its memory; optional printed card.
- Age layers: 7-10 icons + avatar; 11-14 clue balance and certainty words; 15-18 the maths (adding log-odds clues), bias, AI Act.

## 4. Architecture (decided)
- Robot = thin sensor node (Pi 5): camera capture -> MJPEG/HTTP; mic (or USB/wireless close-talk mic) -> 16 kHz PCM over WebSocket; ultrasonic/touch/IMU -> JSON 10-20 Hz; command API for poses, LEDs, TTS playback. Sensor toggles enforced AT THE SOURCE (capture process stopped + lens shutter), so "off" really means no data.
- Stand laptop (offline, dedicated 5 GHz router, no internet): perception (MediaPipe face detector + pose, CLIP ViT-B/32 zero-shot with Platt calibration, k-means colour naming; Silero VAD, faster-whisper multilingual, loudness), evidence memory, fusion, XAI, UI (browser, on the TV), Piper TTS for the robot's voice, optional SD-Turbo imagination, optional printer.
- External/cloud: none. Compared options: (1) mostly onboard (Pi 5 too slow for CLIP+Whisper+diffusion together; privacy good; rejected), (2) distributed local (chosen), (3) hybrid with cloud (rejected: children's data leaves the booth, depends on venue Wi-Fi).
- Measured on the dev laptop CPU (Intel Core Ultra 7 255U, no GPU): CLIP ViT-B/32 94-128 ms/image; SigLIP2 B/16 236-438 ms; faster-whisper small int8 2.3 s per GRID utterance (~2.5 s audio) / 2.4 s per 10 s; per camera frame (face+pose+colour+CLIP) 250-450 ms; SD-Turbo 512 px 1 step ~22 s, 2 steps ~32 s on CPU; LCM img2img via OpenVINO on the integrated GPU 2.6 s (measured; research/06 measured SD-Turbo+TAESD 0.6-0.8 s on the same iGPU; about 1 s or less expected on a mid-range NVIDIA GPU). Piper TTS 1.4 s per sentence. Live mode server (prototype/live/run_live.py) tested on recordings: webcam / PiDog MJPEG URL / file + microphone, sensors start OFF, source-side gating and "forget me" verified.

## 5. Fusion, memory, XAI (implemented, tested)
- Representation = belief over slot values (training-free concept bottleneck).
- Memory = per-visitor in-RAM evidence ledger (list of observations: time, sensor, slot, likelihood, quality, weight, kid-readable evidence). Wiped at session end. No images/audio/transcripts/embeddings stored.
- Fusion = tempered logarithmic opinion pool: score(v) = log prior(v) + sum over enabled sensors of E_m(v); E_m = kappa_m * quality-weighted mean of log(r L(v) + (1-r)/|V|) - log(1/|V|); kappa_m = 1 - exp(-sum q*w / tau). r = slot relevance of the sensor (mixture with "uninformative"), w = independent-evidence units (video frame 0.2-0.3, spoken word 2.0), tau = 1.5. Missing sensor = term omitted = exact marginalisation (never a black frame or silence). Neutral (uniform) priors.
- XAI (faithful by construction): per slot "clue balance" = each sensor's log-odds clues for the chosen answer; in log-odds space the fusion is additive so clue_m = exact Shapley value = leave-one-sensor-out effect = sum of that sensor's evidence cards (unit-tested). Counterfactual "what if" avatars for all 4 sensor configurations. Evidence cards with timestamps. Certainty words (sure / think / guessing / don't know) from rescaled max-probability; disagreement detection ("my senses disagree"). Template sentences only. Rejected for live use: attention maps/rollout, LIME, KernelSHAP/RISE on pixels, MM-SHAP, LLM-written explanations, MC dropout (CLIP has none), evidential DL; optional later: coarse occlusion spotlight after sanity checks (research/07-xai.md).

## 6. Prototype data and results (prototype/experiments/results/)
- Visitors: 6 real people from **Seamless Interaction** (Meta 2025, CC BY-NC 4.0, naturalistic/dev), full-body standing views, 23-38 s segments where they mention an animal; ground truth hand-annotated. Each run clean + "noisy room" (crowd babble from Lombard GRID talkers at 0 dB SNR) + "foggy lens" (blur+dark+noise level 0.7). Faces pixelated on screen.
- Audio evaluation: **Lombard GRID** (CC BY 4.0), 192 utterances, 12 talkers: colour word heard correctly clean 79.7%, wrong colour 0%, missed 20.3%; babble 10 dB 71.4%/0%; 5 dB 64.1%/0%; 0 dB 26.6%/0.5% (missed 72.9%) -> noise makes the robot unsure rather than wrong. Lombard vs plain loudness AUROC 0.81 (absolute level), +7.0 dB within talker. Automatic language ID: 85% detected English on these short command sentences -> at the booth the language is chosen with a flag button.
- Vision evaluation: Open Images V7 human-verified head crops (adults only): glasses AUROC 0.914 (CLIP B/32) / 0.920 (SigLIP2), calibrated ECE 0.084, "I'm sure" claims 92.8% correct; hat AUROC 0.75 (0.83 with a linear probe) -> hat later removed on ethics grounds anyway. CLIP B/32 chosen (better calibrated, 3-4x faster on CPU).
- Ablation on the 6 visitors (final, prototype/experiments/results/ablation_table.md). Clean: eyes+ears know 4.5 of 6 things per visitor, 94% of checkable claims correct (17 claims), animal 5/6, clothes colour 6/6, glasses 5/6; eyes only know 2.3 (animal 0/6, clothes 6/6, glasses 5/6, 100% of 11 claims correct); ears only know 1.8 (animal 5/6, clothes 0/6, glasses 0/6, 83% of 6 claims correct). Noisy room: animal with ears drops 5/6 -> 3/6, eyes+ears still 93% correct but knows 4.2. Foggy lens: clothes colour 6/6 -> 0/6, eyes-only claims only 25% correct (a dark, blurred lens makes clothes look dark: degraded sensors can mislead, not only make unsure). The only unsupported confident claim in clean conditions: Visitor B's favourite colour "brown".
- Annotation lesson: the hand-annotated truth for Visitor C said "no glasses"; the robot said "glasses (think)"; a full-resolution check showed thin transparent frames - the robot was right and the annotator wrong (truth corrected; all glasses labels re-verified on full-res crops). Real failure cases worth showing: Visitor B's favourite colour became "brown" (sure) because she said "ginger brown" about her rabbit and "Snow White" -> keyword spotting has no understanding (in the booth, words count only as answers to the robot's own question); Visitor C's far-field recording: Whisper small AND large-v3-turbo miss "my dog" -> robot honestly says "I don't know" -> argument for the close-talk mic. Recording levels differ by 33 dB between dataset vendors -> loudness needs booth calibration.

## 7. Ethics & law (research/09-ethics-law.md, verified)
- GDPR applies (live processing is processing even without storage). Lawful basis Art. 6(1)(e) public task (SUT's statutory science-dissemination duty) with opt-in buttons as ethical safeguard; Art. 8 does not apply (not an online service). DPIA: do a short formal one with SUT DPO (iod@polsl.pl); no research data collected at SiW.
- AI Act: Art. 5 (since 2 Feb 2025) and Art. 50 (since 2 Aug 2026; Digital Omnibus Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 kept Art. 50 date, moved Annex III to 2 Dec 2027) apply at the fair. No emotion inference (5(1)(f): booth = workplace of staff/teachers; school groups), no biometric categorisation of race etc. (5(1)(g)), Art. 50(1) child-friendly AI disclosure (robot companion pets are explicitly in scope), Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking of generated images, Art. 50(3) avoided by not doing biometric categorisation. Not high-risk if this design is followed.
- Privacy-by-design controls: sensors off by default, physical lens shutter + mic mute, toggles gate data at source, ROI = volunteer mat only, all on-device and offline, RAM only, nothing written to disk, abstract/pixelated display, no face/voice embeddings, attribute allow-list in code, session wipe + "forget me", vendor cloud/BLE disabled, firewall, no staff phones filming children, adult present.
